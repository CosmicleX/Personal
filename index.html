💌 Cram Concept: Anatomy vs. Physiology

Anatomy is like the physical letters Lara Jean wrote and the teal hatbox she keeps them in (it is just the structure and the parts). Physiology is how those letters actually got mailed out and caused a massive chain reaction in her love life (it is how the parts actually function and change things).

Basic Concepts

  • Gross / Macroscopic Anatomy: can be seen by the naked eye (larger structures).
  • Surface Anatomy: overlying skin surface anatomy that focuses on external features.
  • Microscopic Anatomy: cannot be seen by naked eye; includes cytology (cells) and histology (tissues).
  • Developmental Anatomy: structural changes throughout the life span.
  • Pathophysiology: study of functional changes associated with disease and aging.

Anatomical Positions and Planes

  • Standard Position: stand erect, feet together, eyes forward, hands on the side, arms towards the front.
  • Prone Position: faced-down.
  • Supine Position: faced-up.
  • Sagittal plane: divides into left and right.
  • Frontal (Coronal) plane: divides into front and back.
  • Transverse plane: divides into superior (top) and inferior (bottom).

Directional Terms

Superior (cranial): towards the head.
Inferior (caudal): towards the lower part.
Anterior (ventral): at the front.
Posterior (dorsal): at the back.
Medial: toward the middle.
Lateral: outer side / away from medial.
Proximal: close to the origin of the body.
Distal: farther from the origin.
Superficial: towards surface of the body.
Deep (internal): away from surface.
Ipsilateral: on the same side.
Contralateral: opposite side.

🚲 Cram Concept: The 11 Body Systems

The body systems are like everyone in Lara Jean's life working together. The Integumentary system is like Peter Kavinsky putting his arm around her to protect her from school drama. The Cardiovascular system is like Kitty riding her bike to deliver the letters across town. The Nervous system is the communication network of all the gossiping students.

The 11 Organ Systems

  • Integumentary: provides protection, regulates temp, prevents water loss, helps produce vitamin D.
  • Skeletal: protection/support, mineral storage, production of blood cells.
  • Muscular: produces movement and body heat, maintains posture.
  • Lymphatic: removes foreign substances, combats diseases, maintains tissue fluid balance, absorbs dietary fats.
  • Respiratory: exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide, regulates blood pH.
  • Digestive: mechanical and chemical digestion, absorption, elimination.
  • Endocrine: influences metabolism, growth, and reproduction.
  • Cardiovascular: transports nutrients, wastes, gasses, hormones; plays a role in immune response.
  • Urinary: removes wastes, regulates blood pH, ion balance, and water balance.
  • Female Reproductive: produces oocytes, milk, and hormones.
  • Male Reproductive: produces/transfers sperm cells and produces hormones.

Life Characteristics & Needs

Characteristics: Organization / Metabolism / Responsiveness / Growth / Development / Reproduction.
Survival Needs: Nutrients (carbs, proteins, lipids, vitamins, minerals), Oxygen, Water (60 to 80 percent of body weight), Stable Body Temperature (37 Celsius / 98 Fahrenheit), and Atmospheric Pressure.

🏃 Cram Concept: Muscle Movements

Flexion is like pulling a letter close to your chest to hide it (decreasing the angle). Extension is reaching out to hand the letter to someone (increasing the angle). Abduction is stepping away from the awkward situation, and Adduction is coming back together.

4 Primary Tissues

  • Epithelial: covers and lines.
  • Connective: connects and supports. Includes Elastic Cartilage (external ear), Fibrocartilage (cushion discs in vertebrae), Dense Connective (tendons/ligaments), Loose Connective (Areolar, Adipose for fat storage, Reticular), and Blood.
  • Muscle: skeletal (voluntary/attached to bone), cardiac (involuntary/heart), smooth (involuntary/hollow organs/peristalsis).
  • Nervous: receives and conducts impulses.

Types of Body Movements

Flexion: decreases joint angle.
Extension: increases joint angle.
Rotation: movement around longitudinal axis.
Circumduction: moves in a circle.
Abduction: limb away from midline.
Adduction: limb towards midline.
Dorsiflexion: lifting foot to shin.
Plantar Flexion: pointing toes away.
Inversion: sole of foot medially.
Eversion: sole of foot laterally.
Supination: palm faces anteriorly.
Pronation: palm faces posteriorly.
Opposition: moving thumb to touch tips of fingers.

📝 Cram Concept: Epidermis vs. Dermis

The Epidermis is like the fake relationship contract Lara Jean and Peter make, because it is a protective outer layer with strict rules that keeps people out (waterproof and avascular). The Dermis is the real relationship underneath where all the true feelings, blood vessels, and nerve endings actually live.

Skin Regions and Barriers

  • Epidermis: outer layer, keratinized, avascular (no blood). Made mostly of keratinocytes.
  • Dermis: dense connective tissue, vascular (has blood). Red coloring comes from hemoglobin.
  • Chemical Barriers: skin secretions, melanin. Dermcidin in sweat and bactericidal in sebum kill bacteria. Wounds release cathelicidin.
  • Physical Barriers: Stratum corneum (bricks and mortar).
  • Biological Barriers: Dendritic cells, macrophages, and DNA.

Skin Alterations

  • Erythema (Redness): embarrassment, fever, inflammation.
  • Pallor (Blanching): fear, anemia, low blood pressure.
  • Jaundice (Yellowing): liver disorder.
  • Bronzing: Addison's disease.
  • Bruises: hematoma.

Hair and Glands

  • Hair Anatomy: Central Medulla (large cells/air spaces), Cortex (surrounds medulla), Cuticle (outside layer, heavily keratinized). Grows in the matrix of the hair bulb in stratum basale.
  • Associated Hair Structures: Hair follicle, Hair Papilla (supplies nutrients), Arrector Pili Muscle (pulls hair upright when cold).
  • Hair Types/Issues: Vellus hair vs Terminal hair. Hirsutism (excessive hairiness), Alopecia (thinning), Frank Baldness.
  • Sebaceous (Oil) Glands: produce sebum. Imbalances cause whiteheads, blackheads, acne (staphylococcus), and seborrhea (cradle cap).
  • Sudoriferous (Sweat) Glands: Eccrine (palms, soles, forehead - clear sweat) and Apocrine (axillary/anogenital - milky sweat with fatty acids and proteins, activates at puberty).
  • Melanoma ABCD Rule: Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Color, Diameter (larger than 6mm).

🧁 Cram Concept: Bone Cells

Osteoblasts are like Lara Jean baking a new batch of cupcakes from scratch because they build things up, while Osteoclasts are like Kitty sneaking in to eat or smash the cupcakes because they break things down.

Bones of the Human Body (206 Bones)

  • Long Bones: longer than wide, compact bone (e.g. Femur - heaviest/strongest, Tibia - medial shinbone, Fibula - lateral stick-like, Humerus, Ulna - medial/mahaba, Radius - lateral/maikli).
  • Short Bones: cube-shaped, spongy bone (Carpals - 8 wrist bones, Tarsals - 7 ankle bones).
  • Flat Bones: thin, curved (Cranium bones - Frontal, Parietal, Occipital, Temporal, Sphenoid, Ethmoid).
  • Irregular Bones: (Sphenoid, Vertebrae).
  • Sesamoid Bones: (Patella).

Bone Growth and Healing

  • Bone Ossification: Intramembranous (osteoblasts produce bone) and Endochondral (developed from cartilage).
  • Bone Growth: Appositional (growth in width) and Interstitial (grows from within).
  • Repair of Fractures (6 to 8 weeks): 1. Hematoma formed, 2. Fibrocartilage callus formed, 3. Bony callus replaces fibrocartilage, 4. Bone remodeling occurs.
  • Fracture Types: Closed (simple), Open (compound), Transverse, Oblique, Spiral, Comminuted (many small pieces), Impacted, Greenstick (incomplete break).

Joints and Disorders

  • Synovial Joints: have articular cartilage, articular capsule, joint cavity, reinforcing ligaments. Includes Bursae and Tendon Sheaths. Types include Plane, Hinge, Pivot, Condylar, Saddle, Ball-and-socket.
  • Disorders: Giantism, Dwarfism, Osteogenesis Imperfecta (brittle/no collagen), Rickettsia (soft bones), Osteomyelitis (bacterial inflammation), Osteomalacia (decalcifications), Osteoporosis.
  • Spinal Curves: Kyphosis (hunchback), Lordosis (anterior curve), Scoliosis (S curve).

🎀 Cram Concept: Surgical Suffixes

-ectomy means to cut it out and remove it completely, just like taking Gen's scrunchie back so she can never wear it again. -oscopy is just looking at something with a camera, like reading an old love letter without actually throwing it away.

General Medical Roots and Terms

  • Organs: Cardio (heart), Hepato (liver), Nephro (kidney), Chole (gallbladder), Spleen, Pancreas, Small Intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum), Rectum, Anal/Anus.
  • Body Parts: Cephalo (head), Cranium (skull), Optha/Oculo (eyes), Periorbital (around eyes), Auricular/Oto (ears), Nasal/Rhino (nose), Oris/Labia (lips), Oro/Oral (mouth), Buccal (cheeks), Lingual (tongue), Sublingual (under tongue), Mandible (jaw), Mental (chin).
  • Limbs/Regions: Brachial (arm), Antebrachial (forearm), Carpus/Carpal (wrist), Tarsal (foot bone), Femoral (thighs), Patella/Genu (knee), Popliteal (back of knee), Crus (leg), Pedal (foot), Calcaneal (heel), Olcranal (elbow), Pelvis (hips), Perineum/Inguinal (groin), Gluteal (buttocks), Navel/Umbilical, Sternum.
  • States/Conditions: Dolor (pain), Rigor (stiffening after death), Livor (discoloration after death), Algor (decreased temp after death), Emesis (vomiting), Atrophy (decrease in size), Rhythm (rhythmia), Paralysis.
  • Colors: Pallor (pale), Rubor (red), Cyanotic (blue/purple), Gangrene (black/dead), Jaundice (yellow), Leuko (white).

Patient Positions

  • Fowlers: mid sitting 45 degrees. Semi-Fowlers: 30 degrees. High-Fowlers: 90 degrees.
  • Trendelenburg: head lower than feet. Reversed: head higher than feet.
  • Sims: lateral lying with one leg flexed at 90 degrees.
  • Orthopneic: sitting with head reclining on a bedside table.
  • Dorsal Recumbent: lying on back with legs raised on a bed.
  • Lithotomy: lying on back with legs raised on stirrups.
  • Jack Knife: face downwards with hips protruded back exposing anus.

Surgical Procedures

  • Anastomosis: connecting two tubular structures.
  • Craniotomy / Craniectomy: opening skull / removal of skull.
  • TAHBSO: Total Abdominal Hysterectomy with Bilateral Salpingo-Oophorectomy (removal of all reproductive organs).
  • TAH: Total Abdominal Hysterectomy (uterus only).
  • Myomectomy: removal of myoma.
  • Wound Debridement: cleansing a wound.
  • Thoracentesis / Paracentesis: removal of fluid in lungs / abdomen.
  • ORIF: Open Reduction Internal Fixation (repairing fractured bone with titanium plates).
  • Pneumonectomy / Colostomy: removal of lungs / opening in abdomen.
